# OFF TTRPG — Economy Document

Companion to the System Document and Campaign Bestiary. This document defines the item catalog, equipment, currency, and rest structure. Its spine is the attrition budget: at the chosen difficulty, the party fights **7–8 encounters per rest zone**, and everything below is sized against that number.

GM rulings baked in: survival-horror attrition (7–8 fights/leg), full equipment lines, Abaddon's Meat as an uncapped rare drop (supply is GM drop discipline).

## Part 1 — The dial and the pacing

A **leg** is the stretch between rest zones: 7–8 standard fights plus, on boss legs, the boss. One leg is almost exactly one session of play — **rest zones are session boundaries**, and the GM should treat them that way deliberately: the party limps in, spends, restocks, and the session ends on the exhale.

Rest zones restore all HP and CP and cure all statuses. There is no other free recovery anywhere in the system, which is what makes the numbers below matter.

**Placement:** two rest zones per zone (mid-zone and pre-boss), giving each zone two legs. The purified zones run one leg each (three total), the Room runs two, and the finale is fought off the Room's second rest. Campaign total: roughly thirteen legs — thirteen to fifteen sessions of combat play.

## Part 2 — The attrition budget (computed from the bestiary)

Model: the party heals ~45% of incoming damage in combat via competences (tempo healing), covers the rest with items between fights, and rides ~30% of its HP pool as an accepted buffer. Damage competences run in ~60% of fights at ~3 casts each. Drain enemies cost ~8 CP per fight on average where present.

| Leg (at level) | Damage taken / leg | Heal casts (CP) | Total CP burn | Party CP pool | **CP item gap** | **HP item gap** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 (~3) | ~2,300 | 21 (374) | ~680 | 400 | **~280** | **~1,050** |
| Zone 2 (~8) | ~4,300 | 28 (501) | ~800 | 560 | **~245** | **~2,050** |
| Zone 3 (~13) | ~8,500 | 32 (699) | ~1,000 | 730 | **~270** | **~4,200** |
| The Room (~17) | ~11,600 | 39 (848) | ~1,150 | 860 | **~290** | **~5,900** |

Two structural facts fall out. **The CP gap is flat** — roughly 250–290 for the whole campaign, because CP pools grow linearly alongside burn. CP items are therefore a constant-size purchase forever: about five Silver Flesh or two Golden per leg. **The HP gap is the survival-horror axis** — it scales 6× across the campaign, and HP items, not competence healing, are what carry the party through a leg. The Purifier's pool is six heals; the leg needs twenty to forty. Counting tickets is the horror, by design.

Purified-zone legs run hotter: superboss legs should budget +30% on both gaps, and the party should feel that in the shop prices there.

## Part 3 — The item catalog

Canon names and tiers preserved; numbers restatted against the budget **and against individual HP bars** (L20 maximums run 207 Bandit to 380 Burnt — every heal number below was checked against the containers, not just the leg demand). Prices are Zone 1 baseline — see Part 6 for scaling.

| Item | Price (Z1) | Effect | Per-leg usage guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luck Ticket** | 20 | Restores **50 HP** to one character | The small sip — 26–70% of a Zone 1 bar, 13–24% at 20. ~13/leg in Zone 1, supplement everywhere after |
| **Fortune Ticket** | 90 | Restores **200 HP** to one character | The big heal — near-full through Zone 2, 53–97% of a bar at 20. ~8/leg in Zone 2, ~18 in Zone 3, ~24 in the Room |
| **Silver Flesh** | 180 | Restores 50 CP | ~5/leg, all campaign |
| **Golden Flesh** | 480 | Restores 100 CP | The bulk option; 2–3 replace 5 Silver |
| **Joker** | 150 | Revives at **35% max HP**, 50% gauge (system doc overrides canon's 25%) | 2/leg early, 3–4 in the Room and beyond |
| **Moloch's Meat** | 30 | Cures Poisoned | Cheap insurance from Zone 3 on |
| **Belial's Meat** | 100 | Cures all statuses on one character | 2–3/leg from Zone 2 on; the answer to Muted supports |
| **Eye** | 20 | Wide Angle in a bottle | Situational; boss-critical vs Maldicion and the Gnosticī |
| **Inspiration** | 200 | Attack item: 75 base damage + ATK + ESP scaling, no CP | CP-free turns for support classes |
| **Expiration** | 900 | Attack item: 400 base + scaling | The emergency nuke; superboss races |
| **Abaddon's Meat** | not sold | Full party HP+CP restore, all statuses cured. **Out of combat only. Rare drop.** | The parachute — supply is a GM decision, not a cap |

**Abaddon's rule.** Uncapped by system ruling — how many the party ever holds is controlled entirely by how often the GM lets it drop (elites and superbosses, low rates). Using one mid-leg is the panic button that saves a doomed leg. The pressure-valve balance now lives in the GM's drop discipline rather than a cap.

**Item heals versus the Purifier.** A Fortune Ticket (200 HP, costs a turn and money) now sits in genuine tension with Save Second Base (~150, costs a turn and 22 CP) — near-parity per action, with the item paying credits and the competence paying CP. This is the healthiest version of this balance the document has had: items are the between-fights and emergency economy, competence healing is the in-tempo economy, and neither strictly dominates. If playtest still shows the Purifier feeling obsolete, cut Fortune Ticket to 150 (exact Save Second Base parity) before touching anything else.

**Status cures and Cob.** Belial's Meat is the item answer to statuses; Cob's Advent locks items *and* competences precisely because both answers exist. Nothing needs adjusting — just know the fight was designed against this catalog.

## Part 4 — Equipment (full lines, reconciled with the curves)

**Gear is additive on top of the checkpoint tables**, per GM ruling — the tables are true base stats, and equipment is the party's earned edge above them (~15% at full tier). The bestiary was derived against table values; a fully-geared party runs ahead of that math, and the GM compensates with encounter tools where needed. A character a tier behind lacks the edge, and the shop is how you keep it.

**Slots:** five per character, per the canon equip screen — offensive, three defensive, one special. Offensive and defensive-2 are class-locked (Bats+Tunics Purifier; Symbols+Epidermides shared across the ring-bearers; invented Chart/Lockpick and Mold/Mitt lines for Bandit and Burnt); Auras, Colours, and Days are universal pools. **Tiers:** T1 through T4, one per zone, sold by Zacharie in that zone; **T5** pieces exist only as superboss drops and grant the campaign's only over-curve edge (+5%).

Each tier piece carries that class's gear share of the zone's stat growth, split weapon (ATK or ESP, per class scaling) / charm (HP, DEF, or the stat the class leans on). Canon lines assigned from the wiki's Items page: Bats and Tunics to the Purifier, Symbols to all three ring-bearers, Auras to Alpha, Epidermides to Omega, Colours to Epsilon (The Eighth Colour as its T5), Days plus Boxxer's defensive pieces across the Bandit and Burnt charm slots. **All six lines exist as data in off-gear-tables.json**, canon-named throughout except the Bandit and Burnt weapon lines (canon has no lines for them — invented per the flavor here, flagged and renameable); a 16-piece unassigned canon pool rides in the file's meta for GM use. AGI on gear follows the system doc's rarity rule: it appears on exactly one charm line (Lucky Coin's), nowhere else.

Pricing: a full tier for one character costs roughly 600 credits at Zone 1 scale, rising per zone. Gearing six characters is the single largest expense per zone — that is deliberate; it is what the boss-leg income is for.

## Part 5 — Orbs (permanent growth)

Canon's permanent stat items, kept scarce because six players with a shared wallet would otherwise snowball. The discipline is yours via the stock toggle, and the recommended checklist is: **one of each orb per zone** (Taurus +50 max HP, Libra +15 max CP, Scorpio +5 ATK, Gemini +5 DEF, Capricorn +5 ESP), priced at 300 × zone number. **Virgo (+5 AGI) appears exactly twice in the campaign** — once in Zone 3, once in the purified zones — honoring AGI as the rarest stat in the game. Superbosses may drop one bonus orb each. Who gets each orb is a party decision, and arguing about it is content.

## Part 6 — Income, prices, and Zacharie

Income is not a system rule — the GM sets credits and drops per encounter in the Encounter builder. What follows are **income templates**: three bands per zone, anchored to the consumable-basket demand (the one number that IS derived — what a leg costs to survive at the resized catalog). Set encounters at a band; move bands freely as the table's performance dictates. The wallet is shared like the inventory; canon drop credits are discarded.

| Zone | Basket / leg (demand) | LEAN / fight | STANDARD / fight | GENEROUS / fight | Boss payout (×0.8 / ×1 / ×1.2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 | ~1,400 | 175 | 220 | 265 | 2,500 |
| Zone 2 | ~2,600 | 335 | 420 | 505 | 5,000 |
| Zone 3 | ~5,100 | 600 | 750 | 900 | 8,000 |
| The Room | ~8,300 | 880 | 1,100 | 1,320 | 12,000 |
| Purified | ~10,800 | 1,120 | 1,400 | 1,680 | superboss 15,000 |

**What the bands feel like.** STANDARD: the leg's income covers the basket with margins at survival-horror zero in the Room — boss payouts are the gear money. LEAN (~80%): the basket doesn't close — the party sells, skips gear, leans on drops, and feels genuine scarcity; sustainable for a leg or two, corrosive beyond that. GENEROUS (~120%): slack for orbs, gear catch-up, or recovery. Drop guidance per band: LEAN ~1 consumable per 3 fights, STANDARD ~1 per 2, GENEROUS ~2 per 3 with occasional premium drops (Fortune, Joker).

**When to move the dial.** Down a band when the party hoards cash and never sweats a shop visit. Up a band when they enter boss legs under-geared, wipe on standard encounters, or can't afford the basket — and note the wipe penalty interacts here: 50% credit loss usually means running the recovery leg at GENEROUS, which is you administering the mercy the system deliberately doesn't. Prices scale with the tickets: 1× in Zones 1–2, 1.5× in Zone 3, 2× in the Room and purified zones. The basket column is the only fixed truth in this table; everything else is your hand.

**Zacharie's stock per visit** (a suggested checklist — actual stock is the GM's per-visit toggle in the shop's stock gate, so a mid-leg Zacharie can carry three items and a rest-zone Zacharie the full shelf)**:** current gear tier (all classes), tickets and Flesh in leg quantities plus ~30% slack, 3 Jokers, cures, 2 Inspirations, the zone's orbs, and — from Zone 2 on — one Expiration. He appears at every rest zone, because he always does.

## Part 7 — The Bandit's economy

The class that generates supply. **Cutpurse** (+1 drop on Bandit kills): at a ~25% kill share, four to five bonus drops per leg, drawn from the zone's consumable table. **Light Fingers** (5% chance any used item isn't consumed): at ~14 item uses per leg, roughly 0.7 free items — quietly, a ~5% discount on the entire party economy, forever. **Ursa Shot** (16 CP, steal): steals from **the encounter's enemy object pool** — the same GM-stocked pool the enemies themselves use, so stocking a fight's pool is also setting its steal value. Worth casting when the expected item beats 16 CP of value: from Zone 2 on, stock pools with Silver Flesh (break-even) and occasional Fortune Tickets or Inspirations (clear profit) if you want steal turns to happen. Superbosses can each carry one unique steal — the only second-copy T5 accessory, which makes Ursa Shot a turn worth spending in the endgame. All of this is your stocking hand; nothing here is a table the engine owns.

A party without its Bandit for a session runs ~10% poorer. That is the class contribution, priced.

## Part 8 — Interactions ledger

Enemy CP drains (Tiburce, Massu, 笑, Fool's Mate, Dramatic Crescendo) now have a price: every point drained is ~3.6 credits of Silver Flesh — a Massu pack that lands three drains has stolen a Luck Ticket. Sugar's Dummies and Source's Flourish burn the party's *item* clock, not just fight time, which is why those fights sit at the end of legs. A party wipe costs 50% of credits and consumables — roughly a leg's income destroyed — which at this difficulty is the real death penalty. And the finale's Phase 2 arrives after the Room's last leg has already spent the party down — the Bad Batter fight is balanced assuming roughly one leg's slack of items remains, no more. Stock accordingly, or die accordingly.
